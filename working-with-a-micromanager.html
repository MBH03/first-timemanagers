Working for a micromanager.

Most micromanagement isn't a verdict on your work — it's an anxiety response to uncertainty. Close the uncertainty and most of the hovering goes with it.

01
Send the update before they have to ask for it
The less they have to wonder, the less they have to check. A two line update, sent before the question lands, usually buys you more real autonomy than any conversation about trust.

"Quick update: the draft is on track for Thursday, no blockers right now. I'll flag it fast if that changes."

Send it labeled the way Your Boss Never Gets Surprised → sets up: lead with "FYI — no action needed." A micromanager relaxes faster when the label already tells them nothing is required of them, before they've even read the rest.
02
Ask what "done well" looks like to them, specifically
Vague expectations are exactly what a micromanager fills in with constant check-ins. Get their version of done before you start, not after they have already corrected you once.

"Before I start, what does a strong version of this look like to you? I want to build it right the first time."
03
Give them a controlled way to feel involved
A standing fifteen minute sync can absorb the anxiety that would otherwise show up as surprise drive-bys all week. It feels like less control to you. To them, it is less uncertainty.

"Let's grab fifteen minutes every Tuesday so you're never wondering where things stand. I'll come with the update already pulled together."
04
Know a bad habit from a bad fit
Some micromanagers ease up once they have watched you deliver without reminders, a few times in a row. Some never do, no matter what you try.

"What would you need to see from me to give me more room on this?"

If nothing changes after a genuine effort, that is information too, not a personal failure. See Knowing When to Leave → for how to tell the difference.

Run the first three moves for a month before you decide anything about the fourth. If the checking still hasn't changed, the problem was never your communication.

What a Good Month Looks Like

Week 1: you send the Thursday update before they ask, and they still check in twice anyway. Week 2: the standing Tuesday sync starts absorbing the drive-bys, you get one instead of four. Week 3: they skip a check-in because your update already answered it. Week 4: you ask what "done well" looks like on a new project before they weigh in unprompted, and they let you run with it.
