Client Escalation Response Script

The first time a client escalates directly to you, the instinct is to solve the actual problem immediately. Resist that. The first job isn't fixing it, it's making the client feel heard and in control before you've fixed anything, because an unacknowledged escalation gets angrier by the hour, and a rushed, half-right answer usually makes it worse, not better.

Instructions for Managers

Acknowledge fast, ideally within a couple of hours, even if all you have is "I'm on it." Don't promise a fix or a timeline you haven't actually confirmed internally, an over-promise you can't keep costs more trust than the original problem did. Loop in whoever needs to know, your manager, the account owner, before it becomes a surprise for them too. And follow up even when there's no news yet, silence is what turns an escalation into a bigger one.

Copyable Scripts

Three moments, three short messages. Adjust the specifics, keep the shape.

1. Immediate Acknowledgment

"Thanks for flagging this, and I'm sorry for the disruption it's causing. I'm looking into it now and will have a real update for you by [specific time], not just a status check-in. If anything changes before then, I'll reach out sooner."

2. Setting Expectations Once You Know More

"Here's where things stand: [what actually happened, briefly]. Here's what we're doing about it: [the fix, in progress]. Here's what this means for you specifically: [impact, or lack of one]. I'll follow up by [specific time] whether or not it's fully resolved."

3. Internal Loop-In Message

"[Client] escalated [issue] to me directly at [time]. I've acknowledged it and told them I'll follow up by [time]. Wanted you looped in before they mention it to you, here's what I know so far: [summary]. Let me know if you want to be on the next update."

Two Things Not to Do
Don't diagnose live on the call or thread
Guessing at a cause in the moment and being wrong costs more credibility than saying "let me confirm and come back to you" ever does.
Don't let your manager hear about it from the client first
A heads-up costs you two minutes. Your manager finding out from someone else costs you a lot more than that.
