Situation Navigator

The same approach doesn't work on everyone on your team, and that's not favoritism.

Four moves for adjusting how you manage each person without changing what you actually expect from them, not a general essay on personality types.

01
Adjust the delivery, not the standard
One person needs feedback softened, another needs it blunt to actually land: that's fine. What's not fine is quietly expecting less from one of them. The standard stays fixed; only how you communicate it should flex.
02
Learn how each person actually wants to receive feedback
Some people want it the moment something happens. Others want a beat to process before you bring it up. Ask directly instead of assuming everyone wants what you'd want.
03
Notice who needs the plan and who needs the why
Some people just want the steps. Others won't commit until they understand the reasoning behind them. Handing a "why" person a checklist, or burying a "steps" person in context, both slow them down for the same underlying reason: you gave them the wrong kind of information.
04
Keep the fairness test simple: same standard, different door
If someone on the team asked "why does he get to work like that and I don't," you should have an answer that isn't about liking one person more. If you don't have one, that's the sign you've drifted from adapting your style into playing favorites.
