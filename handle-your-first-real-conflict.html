Month 6 · Move 02

Handle your first real conflict directly.

Two reports in a dispute, or a disagreement with your own manager. Don't let it resolve itself — it usually won't, and letting it sit signals that you'll let things sit.

The instinct to hope a conflict cools down on its own is understandable and almost always wrong. It usually just goes quiet on the surface while it keeps accumulating underneath, and it resurfaces later, bigger.

Get both sides before you're in a room together
Talking to each person separately first keeps you from being ambushed by a dynamic you don't understand yet.
Separate the pattern from the specific incident
Your job isn't to rule on who's right about the one incident. It's to name the recurring pattern and get agreement on how to handle it going forward.
Decide what you're mediating versus deciding
Some conflicts need you to facilitate a resolution between two people. Some just need you to make a call. Know which one this is before you walk in.
Follow up — "resolved" once tends to reopen
Put a real date on the calendar to check whether it actually held, not just whether the room felt calmer that day.
