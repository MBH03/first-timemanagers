Situation Navigator

You have to let someone go.

Four moves for the conversation itself, not a general essay on making the decision.

01
Know the decision before you walk in
This conversation is not a negotiation and not the place to reconsider. If there's still a real chance of a different outcome, you're not ready for this meeting yet.
02
Say it in the first thirty seconds
"This is your last day" belongs in the opening line, not buried after ten minutes of preamble. Delaying it is kinder to you, not to them.
03
Keep it short and let HR carry the logistics
This is not the meeting to relitigate performance history. State the decision, cover the practical next steps, and let the details live in a follow-up document.
04
Say something true and specific if you can
A generic "this isn't a reflection of you as a person" lands as hollow. If there's something real and positive you can say, say it. If there isn't, don't manufacture one.
HR Tips

Never get into an argument. If they push back or challenge the decision, don't defend it point by point. Restate that the decision is final and redirect to next steps, arguing implies the decision is still open, and it isn't.

Listen without engaging. Let them talk if they need to. Listening is not the same as negotiating, and it costs you nothing to hear them out.

Keep it short. The longer the meeting runs, the more room there is for it to go sideways. Ten to fifteen minutes is typical, say what needs to be said and hand off to HR.

Run the language by HR or legal beforehand. Don't freelance the wording on the reason for termination, especially anything protected-class adjacent, performance related, or involving a PIP. Have HR review your script before the meeting, not after.

Write it out ahead of time. Don't wing this one. Draft your actual lines in advance, review them with HR, and stick close to what you rehearsed. This isn't the conversation to improvise.
