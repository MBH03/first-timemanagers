AI isn't the problem.
What you do with the time back is.

Old question: "Are you using AI?" New, better question: "What are we doing with the time AI gives back?" AI already makes your 2-hour task take 30 minutes. Your 10 tickets a day can now be 20. If your boss's only move is "cool, do 20," that's not productivity, that's just more busywork with extra steps. The smart move is treating that saved time like a dividend you can invest.

Read this if

Your team is using AI but you're not seeing the payoff, just more volume. You've caught yourself thinking "great, now do more" when someone tells you AI saved them time. You suspect people are quietly sitting on time they've saved instead of telling you. You want a real answer to "what do we actually do with this," not just "adopt more AI tools."

The Problem Managers Are Accidentally Creating

It's simple psychology.

  1. You find AI cuts your task from 2 hours to 20 minutes.
  2. You tell your manager, excited.
  3. Manager: "Great, now do 6 of them."
  4. You learn: being efficient = penalty.

Next time, you don't say anything. You just quietly keep the time. That's not lazy. That's rational. And it's a management failure.

The 3-Bucket Rule

Don't auto-fill saved time with more of the same work. Invest it on purpose.

1. The business — fix the broken stuff
Automate something. Fix the docs. Solve that recurring customer issue. Knock out that project that's been in backlog for six months.
2. The person (you) — level up
Learn SQL. Get better at presenting. Shadow sales or product. Understand another part of the business. This is huge if you're early-career, finishing fast is cool, but what you learn with the extra time is what gets you promoted.
3. The future — experiment
Build a prototype. Test a new workflow. Research a crazy idea. Innovation never happens when everyone is drowning in today's tickets.
A Real Example: Claims Processing

Say someone processes 10 claims a day. AI cuts the time per claim enough that they could now do 20.

The obvious move is to ask for 20. The better move might be: keep the output at 10, and spend the recovered time learning to build and manage an agent that handles claims processing itself.

That's a completely different trajectory. One version has you doing the same job, just faster, forever. The other has you become the person who builds and oversees the system, which is a promotion, not a productivity bump.

This is the real test of the framework: it's not just "what do I do with an extra hour today," it's "does this time go toward doing more of the same work, or toward becoming the person who doesn't have to do it manually anymore."

The 4 Questions Every Manager Needs on a Sticky Note
  1. What work can AI eliminate?
  2. What work can AI make faster?
  3. What should we do with the capacity that creates?
  4. How do we make sure that capacity creates value, not just more activity?

Question 3 is where most managers will fail. We've spent decades trying to get productivity gains. Now that we actually have them, don't waste them by immediately filling every minute with more work.

Final thought

The goal isn't to keep everyone busy for 8 hours. The goal is to create the most value possible in those 8 hours. AI can give us the time. We have to decide not to waste it.

Copy This for Your Next 1:1
If you're the employee

"AI is saving me about [X hours] a week on [task]. I'd like to use that time to [learn Y / fix Z / build W]. Would that be valuable?"

If you're the manager, ask this instead of "are you using AI"

"Where is AI saving you the most time right now? What are you doing with that time? Is there something more valuable you'd want to do with that capacity?"
