Career Advice

Someone just gave you feedback that stung. Here's what to do in the next ten minutes.

Four moves for the moment right after, not a general essay on having a growth mindset.

01
Don't respond in the same conversation
Your first reaction to criticism is rarely your best one. "Let me think about that and come back to you" is a complete sentence, and it buys you the gap between the sting and a response you'll actually stand behind.
02
Separate the delivery from the content
Feedback can be true and also delivered badly: those are two different problems. If you dismiss the message because you didn't like the tone, you lose the useful part along with the part that stung.
03
Ask one clarifying question, not five defensive ones
"Can you give me a specific example?" gets you information. "Well, what about the time when..." gets you a debate. One is you trying to understand the feedback; the other is you trying to win the conversation.
04
Come back with what you're actually going to do differently
Not an apology: a specific change. It's the difference between "you're right, I'll be better" and "I'm going to send the agenda a day ahead of every meeting starting this week." The second one is the only one people actually notice.
