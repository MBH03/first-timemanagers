The first sixty seconds after hard feedback.

You're sitting in the meeting, and someone just said the thing you didn't want to hear. Your face is doing something you can't fully control. Your brain is already building a defense. This is the moment that decides whether the feedback actually changes anything, and it's over before most people even realize it's happening.

Four moves for the moment right after hard feedback lands.

01
Don't respond in the same conversation
Your first reaction to criticism is rarely your best one. "Let me think about that and come back to you" is a complete sentence. It buys you the gap between the sting and a response you can actually stand behind.

"That's worth sitting with. Let me think about it and come back to you tomorrow."
02
Separate the delivery from the content
Feedback can be true and also delivered badly, those are two different problems. Someone being blunt, or catching you off guard, doesn't automatically make them wrong. Deal with the delivery separately, if it's worth raising at all, and judge the content on its own merits.
03
Ask one clarifying question, not five defensive ones
A clarifying question gets you information. A defensive one gets you a debate. "Can you give me a specific example?" is the first. "Well, what about the time when..." is the second.

"Can you walk me through a specific example of when that showed up?"
04
Come back with what you're actually going to do differently
Not an apology, a specific change. "You're right, I'll be better" costs nothing and changes nothing. "I'm sending the agenda a day ahead of every meeting starting this week" is the kind of answer people actually notice.
After the Feedback: A Quick Worksheet

Fill this out in the gap you bought yourself, before you go back with a response.

What was actually said, as close to their words as I can get it (not my interpretation of it)
Is there a delivery issue worth raising separately, or is that not actually the point
One clarifying question I still need answered
The specific, observable thing I'm going to do differently, starting when
