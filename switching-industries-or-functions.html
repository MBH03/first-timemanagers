Career Advice

Switching industries or functions.

A real industry switch runs on translated experience and one real conversation, not reinvention. Four moves.

01
Translate your track record, don't just list it
"Managed a team of six" means nothing without the outcome attached, and it means even less translated into a field that doesn't use your old vocabulary. Rewrite your experience in the language of the field you're moving into, not the one you're leaving.

Instead of "Ran the onboarding process for enterprise clients," try "Built the repeatable process that took new customers from signed contract to first value in under three weeks." The second version works in almost any industry.
02
Lead with transferable judgment, not domain trivia
You will not out-know a domain veteran on day one, and you do not need to try. What transfers is how you diagnose a problem and make a decision under uncertainty, not the acronyms you've picked up.

"I don't know this industry yet, but here's how I'd approach figuring out what's actually driving the number" is a stronger opening than pretending you already know the answer.
03
Get one real conversation with someone who made the jump
Job postings will not tell you what actually matters in the new field. A thirty minute conversation with someone who has already crossed saves you months of guessing.

"I'm exploring a move into [field] from [field]. Would you have twenty minutes to tell me what surprised you most about the switch?" Most people say yes to that question.
04
Expect a demotion in scope before a promotion in trajectory
A lateral or even smaller scope role in the new field is often the fastest real path in, not a step backward. Treat the title drop as the price of admission, not a verdict on your worth.

Have the one conversation before you touch your resume. It will tell you which parts of your story are worth translating.
