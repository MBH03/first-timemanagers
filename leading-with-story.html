Month 6 · Move 05

Leading with story, not just data.

A directive tells your team what to do. A specific example tells them why it's worth doing. By the time you're six months in, that difference is usually what separates a team that complies from one that's actually motivated.

The instinct under deadline pressure is to reach for bullet points: here's the plan, here's the scope, here's the date. Bullet points are efficient, but they only transfer information. When a project is hard or the reason for it isn't obvious, information isn't what's missing — meaning is.

A story does that work instead. Not a polished, rehearsed one — just a specific example, with a real person or situation in it, that shows what the work actually means to someone. "This ships by Friday" is a fact. "The person waiting on this hasn't had a working version in three weeks" is a reason to care about Friday.

What makes an example land isn't length or drama, it's specificity. A vague appeal to "the mission" asks people to feel something on faith. A specific detail — a name, a moment, a before-and-after — gives them something concrete to hold onto instead.

Try this

Before your next team update, swap one slide of bullet points for one real example — a specific customer, a specific teammate, a specific moment — and end it with what it means for the work in front of you right now.
