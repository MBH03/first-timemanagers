Why your team needs SOPs, and how to write ones people actually use.

A process that only lives in one person's head isn't a process, it's a single point of failure with a name attached. Writing it down is what actually lets a team scale past you.

It's easy to resist SOPs early on. They feel like bureaucracy for a team small enough that everyone just knows how things work. That works right up until it doesn't, until the one person who knows the process is out sick, or the team doubles and nobody can repeat the same explanation a sixth time without losing an afternoon to it.

SOPs aren't set in stone. The good ones are living documents that evolve as the process does, not a document written once and forgotten in a drive somewhere. The point isn't to freeze the process, it's to make sure the current version of it lives somewhere other than one person's memory.

Identify
Notice the process you keep explaining from scratch. If you're repeating the same walkthrough to every new team member, that's the one to document first.
Document
Write it as a step-by-step someone could follow with zero extra context, clear stages, action-first steps, and links to the tools or systems involved.
Review
Have someone who didn't write it try to follow it. The gaps they hit are the parts that were only ever clear in your head.
Train
Use the document itself to onboard the next person, instead of narrating the process live again. If it can't carry that weight yet, it's not done.
Monitor
Revisit it when the process changes, not on a fixed schedule you'll forget to keep. An outdated SOP is worse than no SOP, it actively misleads.
Try this

Ask yourself: are you repeating the same explanation to new team members? Whatever answer comes to mind first is the process to document this week.
