A Real Question

My 1:1s turned into status updates. How do I fix that?

They always do. That's the default.

Status updates are safe, easy, and have a right answer. Talking about what's actually stuck, or what you want for your career, is harder. If you don't put guardrails up, both of you will take the easy path every time.

You don't need to ban status updates. You need to put them in a box.

First rule: the employee owns the meeting, not the boss. If you're the manager, you don't own this hour. The person with the lower title does.

That means they set the agenda. They bring the topics. Your job is to show up and be useful, not to run through your checklist.

If you own the agenda, it will become a status update every time, because that's the easiest thing for a boss to ask for.

Make them pre-fill the agenda before the meeting. Don't walk in cold. Put a simple doc or calendar description they have to fill before you meet:

  1. How are you? (anything on your mind)
  2. What's stuck / what questions do you have?
  3. Career / growth

If it's blank, don't cover for them. Start with: “Nothing on the doc, what's actually on your mind this week?” The awkward silence is the point. They'll learn to fill it beforehand.

I run my 1:1s as 15-30-15.

First 15: about you. This is not about work. This is how you learn if someone is burnt out, checked out, or dealing with something.

Show up with one question that can't be answered with a Jira update:

  1. What was the most annoying part of your week?
  2. What do you wish I knew but probably don't?
  3. What's taking up headspace outside of work right now?

Then shut up. If you fill the silence, they'll let you.

Next 30: status or questions. This is where status goes. But contained.

Frame it at the top: “We've got Slack and standup for async updates. Let's use this 30 for whatever is actually stuck.”

That framing matters. You're not asking for a play-by-play. You're asking for where they need you.

When they start listing tickets, redirect kindly:

Say this

“Got it, drop that in the channel so we don't lose it. What's stuck on it?”

If you reward a ticket list with nods and follow-ups, you just taught them this is a status meeting. If you redirect every time, they'll stop bringing lists.

Last 15: career. Never skip this, even when you're busy. If you skip it, you're telling them this meeting is only about this week's output.

One question is enough:

  1. Where do you want to be better in 6 months?
  2. What kind of work do you want more of? Less of?
  3. What's something you want to learn that we haven't made space for?

Write it down. Bring it back next week. That's how they know you meant it.

Give it three weeks. The first two will feel short and a little awkward. The third is where it starts working.
