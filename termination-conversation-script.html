Termination Conversation, Manager Template

This guide helps you lead a termination conversation with clarity, professionalism, and empathy. The focus is on delivering the message clearly, respecting the individual, and making space for a smooth transition. This is one of the hardest conversations a manager has to have, and preparation matters more here than almost anywhere else. Know exactly what you're going to say before you walk in.

Instructions for Managers

Be calm, direct, and respectful. Say the news in the first thirty seconds, don't bury it under preamble. Stick to the facts and avoid unnecessary detail. Let HR manage the logistics and legal follow-up. Maintain confidentiality and professionalism.

Sample Termination Script
1. Opening and Setting Context

"I have to tell you something difficult. Today is your last day with us. Over the past [duration, e.g. three months], we've had conversations about performance expectations and areas needing improvement. You were placed on a Performance Improvement Plan with clear goals, timelines, and support. Despite these efforts, the necessary improvements have not been achieved."

Open by briefly summarizing the prior feedback, then say the news directly. Don't let the context turn into a windup, one or two sentences is enough.

2. Communicating the Decision

"We've made the decision to end your employment with [Company Name], effective [date]. This decision is final and was made after careful review."

State plainly that the decision is final. This isn't the moment to relitigate the history in full, if there's a documented record, one sentence referencing it is enough.

3. Transitioning to HR

"HR will walk you through what happens next. This includes details on your final paycheck, benefits, equipment return, and how to access additional support."

Hand off logistics so you're not fielding detail questions live. You don't need to have every answer, you need to know who does.

4. Opportunity to Respond

"Before we wrap up, is there anything you'd like to ask or share? I'm here to listen. While the decision is final, I want to give you space if there's something you'd like to say."

Give them space to ask questions or share something, without reopening the decision. Let them respond fully, listening isn't the same as negotiating.

5. Closing the Conversation

"[Something specific and honest, if there is one.] I want to thank you for the work you put in here, and I wish you well."

End on something true, not manufactured warmth. If there's something specific and honest you can say, say it. If there isn't, a brief, respectful close is enough on its own, a generic platitude is worse than nothing.

Manager Reminders

Remain calm and confident, don't let emotion, guilt, or discomfort pull you off the message. Be brief, this isn't the moment to debate or revisit the performance history. Use neutral, fact-based language, and avoid blaming or subjective terms like "you failed" or "you didn't care." Avoid personal opinions, stick to documented performance expectations. Let HR manage the rest, they'll cover final pay and logistics. Maintain confidentiality, don't discuss this with the rest of the team, that's not yours to share.

Before This Meeting

Confirm every PIP, coaching note, and prior feedback conversation is documented and has been reviewed with HR. Know exactly what HR will handle afterward, final pay, benefits, equipment return, the separation agreement, and access removal, so you're not guessing live if you're asked.

Legal & HR Considerations
Use fact-based, performance-related language
Avoid emotional or vague statements.
Ensure documentation is complete before the meeting, not after
Every PIP, coaching, and feedback note should already be saved and reviewed with HR.
HR handles
Final pay and benefits
Return of company equipment
Separation agreements or letters
Post-termination access removal
