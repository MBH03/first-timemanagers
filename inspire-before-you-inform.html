Inspire Before You Inform

A playbook for rolling out goals, change, or tough decisions. The science is clear: people forget slides and metrics. They remember stories and how you made them feel. Before you pitch goals, announce a change, or explain a decision, inspire first, inform second.

When you jump straight to the what, brains go to threat mode: "How does this affect me?" A story puts them in trust mode instead: "I understand where this is coming from."

1. Anchor With a Personal Story (5–7 min) — Always Do This

This is not entertainment. It is context for your conviction. Tell a real, personal story, not a company story, that mirrors what you're about to ask of them.

  • Rolling out ambitious goals → a time you were scared to aim high.
  • Announcing change → a time you had to adapt.
  • Tough quarter or a hard decision → a time you chose grit over comfort.

Use this arc: Where I was → What happened → What I learned → Why I'm telling you this now.

Bridge line

"I'm telling you this because that's exactly how I'm thinking about what we're about to do..."

2. Amplify With One Spark (3 min) — Pick a Quote OR a Video

Pick one, never both. One spark is sharp, two is noise.

Use a quote when you want them to think
Read it slowly, twice. Let silence land. Ask: "What does this mean for us?"
Use a video when you want them to feel
Under 3 minutes. Don't over-introduce it, just say "watch this with me," then debrief with one line: "That's the standard."
3. Land the Transition (2 min) — Then Go to the Goals

Connect the dots before you pitch:

Transition line

"That story and that [quote/video], that's the mindset behind what I'm about to share. These aren't just targets. They're [what they represent]."

Then share the goals, change, or decision. Keep it tight, 2 to 3 priorities max.

Quick Picker
Team is anxious about change?
Vulnerable story + Sinek: "Leadership is about taking care of those in your charge."
Team needs to think bigger?
Personal stretch story + Reagan: "Gets people to do the greatest things."
Trust is low?
Story about earning trust + Hemingway: "The best way to find out if you can trust somebody is to trust them."
What Not to Do
  • Don't open with slides or metrics.
  • Don't use both a quote and a video.
  • Don't fake vulnerability, pick a story you actually care about.
  • Don't skip the bridge line, without it the story feels random.

Story makes them listen. Quote or video makes them remember. Then the goals make sense.

Appendix A: Meeting Agenda (30-Minute Rollout)

A minute-by-minute run of the whole meeting, story through close.

0:00–0:02 — Settle In
"Thanks for being here. Before we get into the what, I want to share the why."
0:02–0:09 — 1. Personal Story
Tell your story. Arc: Where I was → What happened → What I learned.
0:09–0:12 — 2. Spark (Quote OR Video)
Read the quote twice, or play the video (under 3 min). One debrief line only.
0:12–0:14 — 3. Bridge
Use the bridge line to connect the story and spark to what's next.
0:14–0:28 — 4. Goals, Change, or Decision
Share 2–3 priorities: what, why it matters, what success looks like. Then Q&A.
0:28–0:30 — Close
Tie back to the story's lesson in one sentence.
Appendix B: Word-for-Word Script

Use this as a starting point, not something to read verbatim. Say it in your own words, but keep the order and the directness.

[0:00] Open

"Team, before I walk through our goals for this quarter, I want to take 10 minutes to share where my head is. Because the numbers will make a lot more sense once you hear this."

[0:02] Story

"A few weeks ago, I was [setup, where you were]. What happened was [struggle or moment]. What stuck with me was [lesson]. I'm telling you this because that's exactly how I'm thinking about this quarter for us." [Pause]

[0:09] Spark, option A: Quote

"I want to leave you with one line that's been sitting with me: '[Quote text]' — [Author]. [Pause 3 seconds. Read it once more, slower.] When I hear that, I think about us and [1-sentence connection]. What does it mean to you?"

Take 1–2 quick responses, then move on.

[0:09] Spark, option B: Video

"I want to show you something short. Just watch it with me." [Play video, under 3 minutes, no preamble.] "Here's why I showed you that: [one-line connection, e.g. 'that's the standard I want us to hold.']" [Pause]

[0:12] Bridge

"That story, and that [quote/video], that's the mindset behind what I'm about to share. These aren't just targets on a slide. They're [what they represent, e.g. 'our chance to prove we can...']. So with that in mind, here are our three priorities..."

[0:14] Goals / Change

"1. [Goal or change #1], why it matters: [one line]. Success looks like: [one line]. 2. [Goal or change #2], why it matters: [one line]. Success looks like: [one line]. 3. [Goal or change #3, optional]. What questions or concerns do you have?"

[0:28] Close

"I'm asking a lot this quarter. But I'm asking it because [tie back to the story's lesson in one sentence]. Let's go do it."

Facilitator Tips
  • Rehearse the story out loud once, 5 minutes max, not 15.
  • Don't open your slide deck until after the bridge.
  • If using a quote, put it on one clean slide, nothing else on it.
  • If using a video, test audio beforehand and have it cued to full-screen.
  • End Q&A by restating the bridge line, bookend the emotion.
