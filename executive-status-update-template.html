Executive Status Update Email Template

This is a different animal from a one-time executive summary or the weekly update you send your own manager. It's a recurring email, going to people several levels above you, most of whom are reading it in the thirty seconds between two other things.

The mistake most first-time managers make here isn't the content, it's the format. They write the update the way they'd explain it out loud: chronologically, with context first and the actual news buried in paragraph three. An executive reading a status email isn't looking to be walked through your week. They're scanning for exactly two things: is anything at risk, and does anything need them. Everything else is optional reading.

Instructions for Managers

Send it on a fixed cadence, weekly or biweekly, even when there's nothing dramatic to report, a missed update reads as worse news than a quiet one. Put the headline in the first line, not the subject line alone. One ask per email, if you have more than one thing you need, that's a meeting, not an email. And don't skip a week just because it was uneventful, an uneventful week is exactly the kind of signal an executive wants on record.

Copyable Template

Subject line: [Team Name] Update — [Date]

Headline[One sentence: on track, at risk, or blocked, and why]
Wins[What shipped or landed since the last update]
Risks / Watch items[Anything that could become a problem, named before it is one]
Metrics[The one or two numbers that matter, and how they moved]
What I need from you[A specific ask, or "Nothing right now"]
Getting the Format Right
Lead with the headline, not the timeline
"On track for the 15th" tells them more in four words than a full paragraph of what happened this week.
Name the risk before someone else does
A risk you flagged reads as good judgment. The same risk, discovered independently a week later, reads as something you were hiding.
Keep the metric the same one every time
Switching what you measure week to week makes it look like you're choosing whichever number looks best, even when you're not.
