How to think about AI.

Vague prompts get vague answers. The fix isn't a better tool, it's treating the conversation like briefing a new hire: who's involved, what the actual problem is, and what you want out of it.

Weak prompt

"Give me ideas to motivate my team." You'll get "recognize good work, set clear goals, build team spirit," a list generic enough to apply to any team anywhere, which means it's genuinely useful to none of them.

Strong prompt

"I manage a 12-person remote sales team struggling with low morale after missing Q2 targets. Suggest five creative strategies to boost motivation during our weekly virtual meetings, including tools we can use." Same question, but now it has a team, a problem, and a shape for the answer to take.

Talk to it, don't just paste a question in
A back-and-forth beats a one-shot query almost every time. Treat the first answer as a draft, not the final word.
Ask it to coach your prompt, not just answer it
Got a flat answer? Ask directly: "That answer was too broad, how should I rephrase the question to get something more specific?" It's like having a mentor who knows exactly how to ask.
Have it rate what you asked
"Rate my prompt 1 to 10, and tell me how to make it better." Turns every question into a small lesson in asking better ones next time.
Keep some work the old-fashioned way
Not everything needs it. Doing some thinking without any help keeps your own instincts from going soft, and it's still where your best ideas will come from half the time.

A decent answer usually isn't the end of the conversation. Ask a follow-up: "can you give real-world examples of how this worked?" or "give me a step-by-step plan to actually do this." The difference between a forgettable answer and a genuinely useful one is often just one more question.

Manager's prompt cheat sheet

Idea generation: "What are [number] creative ways to [goal] for a [team type] team dealing with [challenge]? Include real-world examples."

Coaching: "I asked [your prompt]. Rate it 1-10 and suggest how to improve it for more specific, actionable answers."

Follow-up: "Can you provide examples or a step-by-step plan for how [suggestion] has worked for other managers, with measurable results?"
