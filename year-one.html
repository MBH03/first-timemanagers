Stage 04 · Year 1

Making It Yours

Moving from surviving the role to actually shaping how your team works.

First, the part that's easy to skip past: you made it a year. You've run a review cycle, handled a real conflict, probably delivered at least one piece of bad news, and your team is still here and still working. That's not nothing. If you haven't already, this is the point to consciously shift from managing day to day to managing where the team is headed over the next six to twelve months, and to start being deliberate about how you want them to work, not just what they're working on.

01 Decide what you'd change if it were fully yours By year one, you have enough standing to actually make calls, not just execute the ones you inherited. Start being deliberate about which of those inherited habits you keep. Read → 02 Build the thing nobody assigned you The best first-year managers usually build one process, ritual, or system nobody asked for and everyone ends up using. That's the clearest signal the role is actually yours now. Read → 03 Start developing your own manager Someone on your team may be close to where you were a year ago. Start treating their growth as part of your job, not a distraction from it. Read → 04 Write down what you'd tell yourself on day one Not for anyone else, it's how you notice how much has actually changed, and what you'd genuinely do differently if you were starting over today. Read → 05 Decide what kind of manager you want to be known as You've had enough time to know what's actually you versus what you copied from someone else early on. Be deliberate about which parts you keep. Read → 06 Audit your own habits, not just your team's Some of your own routines from month one are outdated by now. Look at your own operating patterns as critically as you'd look at anyone else's. Read →
