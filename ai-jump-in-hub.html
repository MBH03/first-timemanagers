AI Hub · Step 1 of 8

Stop and learn AI. You don't need to be an expert, you need to start.

If you've heard everyone talking about AI but still aren't sure how you're supposed to use it at work, start here. You don't need to understand how models work or learn the terminology. You need to understand one thing: AI can help with a lot of the work that already fills your day. This page is your first 15 minutes with it.

Forget Everything You've Heard

There's a lot of noise around AI: models, agents, tokens, context windows. None of it matters yet. For now, think of AI as a very capable assistant who has read an enormous amount, writes quickly, and can organize a messy problem, but doesn't know your company, your team, or your situation unless you tell them.

You provide the context and the judgment. AI helps with the work. That's the whole relationship.

What a Manager Actually Uses It For

Forget theory. These are things you already do, AI is just another way to do them.

Prepare for a difficult conversation
"I need to talk with an employee who has missed several deadlines. Help me prepare. Ask me questions before giving me advice."
Understand something
"Explain this policy to me like I'm a new manager. Then give me the five things I actually need to remember."
Write something
"Here's the email I wrote. Make it clearer and more direct, but keep my voice and don't make it sound corporate."
Analyze something
"Here are the last 30 customer complaints. What patterns do you see? Separate the common problems from the unusual ones."
Think through a decision
"Here's what we're doing today and why I'm considering a change. Challenge my thinking and tell me what I might be missing."

None of these require you to be an AI expert. They're just things managers already do.

Let's Try It

Don't just read this page, open a tool. If you already have ChatGPT, use that. Microsoft shops often have Copilot, Google shops often have Gemini. Claude is another popular option. You don't need to spend money right now, pick one and open a new conversation.

Paste this real management problem into it:

Paste This

"I manage a team of 10 people. Our weekly team meeting has become a 60 minute status update. I don't think it's very useful anymore, but I don't want to eliminate it because there are still important issues the team needs to discuss. Help me think through how I could change the meeting. Before you give me recommendations, ask me five questions about my team and what I want the meeting to accomplish."

Read the response. Answer its questions. See what it recommends. You just did something important: you didn't ask AI a trivia question, you gave it a real problem and asked it to help you think.

Don't Take the First Answer

This is probably the biggest shift in how you need to think about AI. Your first question doesn't have to be perfect, and neither does its first answer. Talk to it. If you don't like the response, say so: "That's not quite what I'm looking for, I want it shorter but I still want time for the team to raise problems, try again." Then: "Give me three different structures instead of one recommendation." Then: "What could go wrong with each option?"

You're having a conversation, not filing a request into a box and waiting for output.

The 5-Step Method

Once you're past your first try, this is the shape worth remembering. Don't ask it like a search engine, give it a job, with guardrails.

  1. Name the job. Understand, draft, compare, or decide the next step, not "help me."
    Example: "Draft a first-pass answer to this customer question so I can edit it."
  2. Add context. Who it's for, what good looks like, constraints, and one or two real examples.
    Example: "Audience: a new manager. Tone: calm, no hype. Here are two examples of what I mean: [paste]."
  3. Protect private info. Replace sensitive data with labels before you paste anything.
    Example: use [account number] and [customer name] instead of the real values.
  4. Ask for uncertainty. Make it tell you what could be wrong.
    Example: "What could be wrong, outdated, biased, or unsafe in this answer? Give me a checklist."
  5. Verify before acting. Prices, dates, laws, schedules, open the official page yourself.
    Example: no copy-paste to a customer until you've checked the source.

One Warning

AI is incredibly useful, it's also capable of being confidently wrong. Don't blindly copy and paste whatever it gives you. If it hands you a date, price, law, or statistic that matters, verify it. If you're working with confidential company, customer, or employee data, follow your company's policy before pasting it in. You're still responsible for the work, AI is helping you do it.

Your First Assignment

Before moving on to Step 2, do these three things:

  1. Use AI on one real problem. Something you're actually working on, not a toy example.
  2. Have a conversation with it. Don't stop after the first answer. Give it more information, challenge it, ask it to try again.
  3. Notice what you repeat. Pay attention to the work you do again and again, a status update, a certain kind of email, the same customer question. That's what you'll build on in Step 5.

Don't worry about agents yet. Don't worry about automation. For now, just use it. Every time you sit down to do something repetitive or hard to organize, ask: could AI help with this? Try it, see what happens.

You don't need to learn everything about AI. You just need to learn how to use it. Then we'll find the parts of your job it can actually help you do better, faster, or differently.
