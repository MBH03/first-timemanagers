Stage 01 · Week 1

Getting Oriented

Congratulations on your promotion. This is a huge milestone in your career. Here are a few ideas to help you through the first week. Remember, while you're excited and ready to jump in, you first want to take a breath, learn, talk, and plan.

What to actually do in your first five days, what to deliberately not touch yet, and how to introduce yourself in a way that earns trust instead of just announcing a title.

Were You Already On This Team?

Most of the moves below still apply either way: learning the workflow from a manager's view, saying what you don't know yet out loud. The introduction is the one place it really changes, so the Team Introduction section further down splits into two paths: 1.a for a former peer team, 1.b for a team that's actually new to you.

01 Listen before you diagnose Spend the week watching, not deciding. Sit in on real meetings, read how work actually moves, resist the urge to fix anything, you don't have enough context yet to know if it's actually broken. Read → 02 Meet every person on your team 1:1, not as a group Ask what's working, what's not, and what they wish someone had told them when they started. You'll hear things in a 1:1 that never surface in a team meeting. Read → 03 Learn the actual workflow, not the org-chart version Sit in on a real piece of work getting done and watch where it gets stuck. The org chart tells you who's involved. It doesn't tell you where the friction actually is. Read → 04 Say what you don't know yet, out loud Naming that you're still learning builds more trust than pretending you already have it figured out. Your team already knows you're new, pretending otherwise just costs you credibility. Read →
Team Introduction

How you open this depends on whether the team already knows you. Pick the path that fits.

1.a · Managing a Former Peer Team

Skip the getting-to-know-you framing, they already know you. Name the shift directly instead, once, and move on.

What to Say

"I know this is a little different now that I'm your manager. I'm still figuring out exactly what that looks like day to day, but here's what I don't want to change: [something true, e.g. I still want you to push back when you disagree with me]. Here's what will be different: [something true, e.g. I'm the one who signs off on X now]." Then open it up: "What questions do you have about how this changes things?"

Name it once, briefly, then let it go
One direct conversation beats weeks of everyone feeling the shift and nobody saying it out loud.
Don't over-explain or over-apologize for the promotion
It happened. Dwelling on it reads as uncertain, not humble.

1.b · New Manager Introduction

For a team that's actually new to you. Here's an agenda and a script outline you can adapt, add pictures of your family, your hobbies, your style. It makes you feel human and helps people remember. Have fun with it.

What to Say: Script Outline
The Warm Greeting
"Hi everyone, I'm [Name], and I'm really excited and honored to join you as our new [Role/Manager]."
You Outside of Work
Hobbies, family, etc.
Your Background (Briefly)
"To share a little about me, I've spent the last [Number] years working on [mention 1-2 relevant past experiences or skills], but I know I have a lot to learn about how things work here."
Your Approach
"My main priority right now isn't changing things, it's getting to know all of you, understanding what you do, and figuring out how I can best support your success."
The Invitation
"I'd love to hear a bit from each of you about your roles, what you love working on, and anything you think I should know as I get started."
Suggested Meeting Agenda (30–45 Minutes)
SegmentWhat happens
Welcome and Intro (10 min)State your name, role, and a quick personal or professional highlight.
Team Introductions (10–20 min)Ask team members to share their names, roles, and a fun fact or how long they've been with the company.
Open Discussion & Listening (15 min)Ask open-ended questions to understand their current state.
Next Steps (5 min)Outline plans to schedule individual 1-on-1 chats with each team member.
