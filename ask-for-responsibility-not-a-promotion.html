Ask for responsibility, not just a promotion.

When you speak with your boss about your career, do not focus only on the title. Ask for opportunities that will help you develop the skills the title requires.

You might say: "I want to move toward management. What responsibility could I take on that would allow me to practice leading people or coordinating work?" That question is easier for a manager to answer than, "When will I be promoted?" It also shows that you understand advancement is connected to capability.

Your boss may ask you to lead a training session, coordinate a project or mentor a new employee. Treat the opportunity seriously. Clarify what success looks like. Ask how you will be evaluated. Request feedback afterward. Then use what you learn to improve.

A single leadership assignment will not prove that you are ready to manage. A pattern of taking responsibility, learning from experience and improving over time might.

Do not ask for responsibility in a vague way. Make it easier for your manager to say yes by proposing a specific opportunity. For example, you might suggest owning the next onboarding cycle, coordinating a cross-functional project or creating a process improvement plan.

A strong request includes three parts:

  1. The career goal
  2. The responsibility you want to practice
  3. The way success will be measured

This turns ambition into a practical development conversation.

You should also ask about the limits of the assignment. Will you have authority to make decisions? Who needs to be consulted? What resources are available? What happens if priorities change? The more clearly the assignment is defined, the more useful the experience will be.

Career Conversation Template

Use this script with your manager.

"I am interested in moving toward management. I would like to understand which skills I need to strengthen. What responsibility could I take on that would help me practice leadership?"

Then ask:

"What would success look like?"
"How will you evaluate my performance?"
"What decisions would I be able to make?"
"Who would provide support if I get stuck?"
"What should I do differently next time?"
"When should we revisit my progress?"
"What would make you more confident in my readiness?"

Specific Request Template

Propose the opportunity yourself instead of leaving it open-ended.

"I would like to practice coordinating work across the team. The next [project or process] seems like a good opportunity. I could create the timeline, clarify ownership, run weekly updates and provide a final summary. Would you be comfortable letting me own that coordination? We could define success as completing the work by [date], keeping responsibilities clear and receiving feedback from the participants."

Tip: Ask for a Development Plan

If your manager is supportive, ask for a simple plan with: one leadership responsibility, one skill to improve, one measurable outcome, one feedback date, one follow-up conversation, one possible next opportunity. Write the plan down after the conversation. A written summary prevents misunderstandings and gives you something concrete to review.

Follow-Up Template

Send this after completing the assignment. It demonstrates reflection and makes your development visible.

"Thank you for the opportunity to lead [assignment]. We completed [result]. I believe [specific action] worked well, while I could improve [specific area]. I would appreciate your feedback on my communication, judgment and follow-through. What should I continue doing, and what should I change in the next opportunity?"
