Negotiating a raise or a job offer.

The conversation where money is on the table rewards preparation. Four moves, and the exact words for each.

01
Know your number before you're in the room
Decide your target and your walk-away number in advance, in writing, so you are not deciding live under pressure. The room is not the place to figure out what you actually want.

Write both down beforehand: "Target: the number that reflects the work I'm doing now. Walk-away: the number below which the answer is no, not 'let me think about it.'"
02
Let them name a number first
Whoever anchors first gives away information. If you are asked for a number before they have given one, redirect once before you answer.

"What's the range you're working with for this role?"
03
Ask for the whole package, not just salary
Base is one line item. You have more room to negotiate across the whole package than on one number alone.

"Before we finalize, can we look at the whole package together, base, bonus, equity, and start date, not just the one number?"
04
Get it in writing before you say yes anywhere else
A verbal agreement is not a plan. Wait for the written offer before you resign from anything, turn down anything else, or tell your current manager you are leaving.

"This all sounds great. Could you send it over in writing so I can review the details before I give notice anywhere?"
The Ask

"I've really enjoyed the work on [project], and over the last six months I've [specific result, e.g., cut onboarding time by 20% and taken on two direct reports]. Based on what similar roles are paying, I'd like to talk about bringing my salary to [target]. Can we discuss it?" Then stop talking. Let the silence sit instead of filling it with a discount on your own ask.

If They Say Not Now

"Not now" isn't "no" if you don't let it drop there. Ask what would need to be true for the answer to change, get an actual date on the calendar to revisit it rather than an open-ended "later," and send a short summary email afterward so there's a record of what was said and what happens next.

"Understood. What would need to be true for this to be a yes? Can we put a specific date on the calendar to revisit it, rather than leaving it open-ended?"

Then follow up in writing the same day: "Thanks for the conversation today. To recap: we agreed to revisit [target/ask] on [date], contingent on [whatever was named]. I'll check back in then."
