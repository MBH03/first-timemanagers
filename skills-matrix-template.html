The Skills Matrix Template

One table that shows you who to call when someone's out, and where you have no backup at all. A skills matrix lists your team down one side and the skills their work actually requires across the other, then rates every person against every skill on a simple scale.

Most teams only find out about a skills gap when the one person who could do something goes on leave, or leaves the company. A matrix turns that into something you can see coming, and plan for, months in advance instead of finding out the hard way.

What It's Good For
Training and development
Turns vague "I should grow" conversations into a specific next skill to build, with a visible before and after.
Cross-training and backups
Any skill with only one person above novice is a single point of failure. The matrix makes that risk impossible to miss.
Hiring gap analysis
When you're staffing a new role, the matrix tells you whether you're hiring to fill a real gap or just hiring a familiar profile again.
Project staffing
Assigning a new project gets faster and more accurate when you can see who actually has the right skill, not just who has the free time.
How to Build One
  1. List the skills that actually matter. Pull from real work, not a generic competency list. If nobody's used a skill in six months, question whether it belongs on the matrix.
  2. Define a simple scale with plain definitions. A 0 to 4 scale works well. Write one plain sentence for what each level looks like in practice, not just a label.
  3. Self-rate first, then calibrate with the manager. People are often harder on themselves than a manager would be, or the reverse. The conversation matters more than the number.
  4. Look for single points of failure. A column with only one person at level 3 or higher is a risk, not a strength, flag it before it becomes an emergency.
  5. Turn it into development plans and a hiring brief. Every gap you find becomes either something to train internally or something to hire for next.

Quick Tips
Do
  • Keep the scale to five levels or fewer, more than that and ratings stop being consistent between people.
  • Revisit it twice a year, or after a big project wraps.
  • Use it out loud in one on ones, not just filed away after a single pass.
Don't
  • Don't tie it directly to compensation, that makes every rating defensive instead of honest.
  • Don't list every possible skill, a matrix with forty columns gets filled out once and never again.
  • Don't let a gap sit for a year once you've found it, that's the whole point of finding it.
