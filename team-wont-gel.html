Situation Navigator

When the team isn't the problem, but something's still off.

Every individual is doing fine work. Meetings happen, deadlines mostly get hit, nobody's obviously struggling. And yet something about the team, together, doesn't feel like it's clicking. Four moves for diagnosing that gap before you reach for a fix that solves the wrong problem.

01
Check whether it's a trust problem or a clarity problem
Teams that don't trust each other look identical, from the outside, to teams that don't understand who owns what. They need completely different fixes, a trust problem needs psychological safety work, a clarity problem needs cleaner ownership, so diagnose which one you're actually looking at before you act.
02
Create one shared win early
A small, visible thing the whole team accomplishes together does more for cohesion than a values exercise or an offsite ever will. Pick something real, not manufactured, an actual deliverable the team can point to and say "we did that."
03
Address the one person quietly checked out
A single disengaged person can flatten a team's energy more than any process issue. Everyone else feels it before you name it, and the longer it goes unaddressed, the more it reads as something you're choosing not to see. Deal with it directly instead of hoping the team culture fixes it on its own.
04
Model the behavior you want mirrored
Teams gel around what their manager visibly does, not what their manager says they value. If you want candor, be visibly candid first, in a meeting, where they can see it. If you want people to admit mistakes, admit one of your own before you ask them to.
Team Diagnosis Worksheet

Fill this out honestly before you act. The fix only works if the diagnosis is right.

What specifically feels off, in concrete terms, not just a vague sense
Is this trust, clarity, or something else entirely
Is there one person driving most of the friction
What would a shared win look like in the next two weeks
What am I modeling right now that I'd rather they not mirror
