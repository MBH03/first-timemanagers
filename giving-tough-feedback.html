You need to give feedback that's going to sting.

Four moves for the conversation itself — not a general essay on feedback culture.

01
Say it in the same week you notice it
Feedback that waits a month has lost its context and gained resentment. The longer you sit on it, the more it curdles into something bigger than the original issue.
02
Separate the behavior from the person
Describe what happened, not what you've concluded about their character. "You missed the deadline" lands differently than "you're not reliable."
03
Say the actual sentence you're avoiding
If you can't imagine saying it plainly out loud, you're probably about to soften it into something they'll miss entirely. Practice the blunt version first.
04
Don't apologize for the feedback itself
Apologizing for delivering necessary feedback teaches people to expect it to be optional. You can be kind without treating honesty as a favor.
