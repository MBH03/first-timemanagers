Career Advice

Managing up, without losing yourself.

Four moves for working well with your own manager, not a general essay on office politics.

01
Learn what your manager actually gets measured on
Your priorities land better when you can connect them to what they're accountable for. Ask directly if you don't already know.
02
Bring solutions, not just problems
Show up with a recommendation, even a rough one. It's easier for a manager to react to a proposal than to generate one from scratch on your behalf.
03
Ask for feedback before it becomes a review surprise
Don't wait for the formal cycle to find out what's not landing. Ask directly and often: a surprise in a review is usually a sign you weren't asking enough.
04
Say no to the wrong things, not just yes to everything
Managing up includes protecting your own time and your team's. A well-reasoned no builds more trust over time than silent overcommitment does.
05
Send a weekly update, even if nobody asked for one
A manager who has to ask "what's going on with your team" has already lost visibility they should have had by default. A short, consistent update prevents that question from ever needing to be asked.
06
Name your projects so a stranger could follow them
A project called "Phase 2" means nothing to someone two levels up. A project called "Checkout Speed" gets remembered, referenced, and asked about later, which is most of what visibility actually is.
07
Celebrate wins loudly, not just inside the team
Recognizing your team privately is necessary but not sufficient. Say it in the update, say it in the channel your manager reads, say it where the people who decide what happens next can actually see it.
A simple weekly update

Wins: [what shipped or landed this week]. In progress: [what's actively moving, and when it's expected]. Blockers: [anything you need from someone above you]. Coming up: [what's next].
