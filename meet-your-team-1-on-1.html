Week 1 · Move 02

Introduce yourself to the team, then meet every person 1:1.

How you open this depends on whether the team already knows you. Either way, the individual 1:1 is where the real information surfaces, not the group meeting.

A group setting produces the version of the truth people are comfortable saying in front of each other. A 1:1 produces the version they're actually thinking. That gap is usually where the real information lives.

Pick Your Path
1.a · New to This Team

Open with a short group introduction before the 1:1s start. Yes, it's the polite thing to do, but it's also your first real look at how the team works together: who speaks first, who defers, who stays quiet until asked directly. Notice it. Don't comment on it yet.

What to Say: Script Outline
The Warm Greeting
"Hi everyone, I'm [Name], and I'm really excited and honored to join you as our new [Role/Manager]."
You Outside of Work
Hobbies, family, etc.
Your Background (Briefly)
"To share a little about me, I've spent the last [Number] years working on [mention 1-2 relevant past experiences or skills], but I know I have a lot to learn about how things work here."
Your Approach
"Right now my main priority is getting to know all of you, understanding what you do, and figuring out how I can best support your success. Changing things can wait."
The Invitation
"I'd love to hear a bit from each of you about your roles, what you love working on, and anything you think I should know as I get started."
Suggested Meeting Agenda (30–45 Minutes)
SegmentWhat happens
Welcome and Intro (10 min)State your name, role, and a quick personal or professional highlight.
Team Introductions (10–20 min)Ask team members to share their names, roles, and a fun fact or how long they've been with the company.
Open Discussion & Listening (15 min)Ask what's working, what's not, and what you should know before you start changing anything.
Next Steps (5 min)Tell them when you'll be scheduling their individual 1:1.

Then meet each person 1:1, below.

1.b · Already Knew This Team

Skip the group intro, they've sat through enough of your meetings already. Name the shift once, briefly, then move straight into the 1:1s below: "I know this is a little different now that I'm your manager. Here's what I don't want to change, and here's what will be." First 1:1s, One at a Time
Ask about their story, not just their role
How someone got here tells you what they value. It's context you won't find in a file.
Ask how they want feedback before you ever have to give any
Some people want it blunt and immediate. Some want room to arrive at it themselves. Find out now, not in the moment you need it.
Ask this in your 1:1s

"What made you take this role in the first place?" · "What do you wish someone had told you when you started?" · "What's the best way for me to give you feedback?"

First 1:1 Agenda, Getting to Know You

Keep it light, this is not an interview.

1. Introduce Yourself

Give them the short version of how you got here, not the whole résumé.
Ask: "What aspects of your role are you most passionate about?"

2. Hobbies and Interests

Ask: "What do you like to do outside of work? Anything you're particularly into right now?"

3. Preferences

Ask: "What do you enjoy most about your role? What's getting in your way most often?"
"If you could change one thing about how we work, what would it be?"

4. Past Experience

Ask: "What did you do before this? Anything from that work you think would actually help here?"

5. Wrap-Up

Thank them for the time, and tell them one specific thing you heard that you're going to follow up on.
